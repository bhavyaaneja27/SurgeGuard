# SurgeGuard Policy Simulation Engine Specification

## 1. Overview & Purpose

The **What-If Policy Lab** enables operational leaders and market designers to simulate counterfactual interventions against synthetic marketplace shocks generated by the SurgeGuard Digital Twin.

By anchoring comparisons to the exact baseline scenario run (sharing starting fleet size, passenger arrival rates, and random seed), the simulator measures the isolated causal impact of platform interventions on market clearing, passenger waiting times, fare surges, and platform expenditure.

---

## 2. Supported Policy Interventions

| Policy Identifier | Policy Name | Key Parameters | Target Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `no_intervention` | Baseline Control | None | Observe organic clearing without platform financial intervention. |
| `targeted_driver_incentive` | Targeted Driver Incentive | `incentive_amount_pct` (5%–100%) | Attract and reposition idle/adjacent drivers to the affected zone. |
| `rider_discount` | Rider Fare Discount | `discount_amount_pct` (5%–50%) | Stimulate passenger booking demand and protect brand loyalty. |
| `supply_recovery_support` | Supply Recovery Support | `recovery_support_pct` (10%–60%) | Provide a targeted revenue floor to prevent driver offline churn during shortages. |

## 3. Mathematical Formulations & Elasticities

### 3.1 Targeted Driver Incentive
Driver supply responds to per-ride incentive bonuses $\alpha \in [5\%, 100\%]$ with empirical supply elasticity $E_s = 0.50$:

$$S_t = S_{0, t} \times \left(1 + \min\left(0.55, E_s \times \frac{\alpha}{100}\right)\right)$$

- **Demand Effect:** Passenger demand remains at baseline $D_t = D_{0, t}$.
- **Platform Cost Formulation:**
  $$\text{Cost}_{\text{incentive}} = \sum_{t} C_t \times F_{\text{base}} \times \left(\frac{\alpha}{100}\right)$$
  where $F_{\text{base}} = \$18.50$ (benchmark trip fare) and $C_t$ is completed rides in step $t$.

### 3.2 Rider Fare Discount
Passenger demand responds to price discounts $\beta \in [5\%, 50\%]$ with price elasticity of demand $E_d = 0.60$:

$$D_t = D_{0, t} \times \left(1 + \min\left(0.40, E_d \times \frac{\beta}{100}\right)\right)$$

- **Supply Effect:** Available drivers remain at baseline $S_t = S_{0, t}$.
- **Platform Cost Formulation:**
  $$\text{Cost}_{\text{discount}} = \sum_{t} C_t \times F_{\text{base}} \times \left(\frac{\beta}{100}\right)$$

> **CRITICAL MARKET DEFICIT WARNING:**
> When applied during an acute supply shortage, price discounts stimulate passenger booking requests without expanding vehicle capacity. This increases unfulfilled requests and lengthens passenger waiting queues ($W_t$), making rider discounts counterproductive during driver deficits.

### 3.3 Supply Recovery Support Floor
Activates conditionally only when the supply-to-demand ratio $R_{0, t} = \frac{S_{0, t}}{\max(1, D_{0, t})}$ falls below the shortage threshold of `0.85`:

$$S_t = S_{0, t} + \max\left(0, (S_{\text{norm}} - S_{0, t}) \times \min\left(0.55, 0.70 \times \frac{\gamma}{100}\right)\right)$$

- **Platform Cost Formulation:**
  $$\text{Cost}_{\text{recovery}} = \sum_{t \in \text{shortage}} S_t \times \$4.50 \times \left(\frac{\gamma}{30.0}\right)$$
  Incurred exclusively during shortage intervals to protect driver revenue floors.

## 4. Market Clearing & Dynamic Pricing Physics

All policy regimes calculate dispatch fulfillment, unfulfilled demand, and fare pressure identically:

1. **Dispatch Capacity:** $\text{Max Dispatchable} = S_t \times 0.94$.
2. **Completed Rides:** $C_t = \text{int}(\min(D_t, S_t \times 0.94))$.
3. **Unfulfilled Requests:** $U_t = \text{int}(\max(0, D_t - C_t))$.
4. **Supply-Demand Ratio:** $R_t = \frac{S_t}{\max(D_t, 1.0)}$.
5. **Fare Pressure Index ($FP$):**
   - If $R_t < 1.0$:
     $$FP_t = 100.0 + \min\left(220.0, 110.0 \times \left(\frac{1.0 - R_t}{R_t + 0.15}\right)^{1.15}\right)$$
   - If $R_t \ge 1.0$:
     $$FP_t = \max(88.0, 100.0 - 12.0 \times \min(1.0, R_t - 1.0))$$
6. **Passenger Wait Time ($W_t$ in minutes):**
   - If $R_t < 1.0$:
     $$W_t = 3.5 + \min\left(18.0, 9.5 \times (1.0 - R_t)^{1.3}\right)$$
   - If $R_t \ge 1.0$:
     $$W_t = \max(2.2, 3.5 - \min(1.2, 0.8 \times (R_t - 1.0)))$$

## 5. Cost-Effectiveness Metric

The simulator evaluates the marginal cost-efficiency of interventions via:

$$\text{Cost per Recovered Ride} = \frac{\Delta \text{Platform Cost}}{\Delta \text{Completed Rides}} = \frac{\text{Cost}_{\text{intervention}} - 0}{C_{\text{intervention}} - C_{\text{baseline}}}$$

This allows operators to assess whether high-expenditure incentives or targeted floor guarantees deliver superior return on investment.
